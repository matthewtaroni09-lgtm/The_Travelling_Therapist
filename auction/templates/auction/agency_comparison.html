Agency-Based Staffing Models in Canada

Digital Platforms + Brick-and-Mortar Recruiters
Model Overview

When clinics and care homes need coverage quickly, staffing agencies are often the first call. In Canada, these agencies now appear in two formats: traditional recruiter-led firms and modern app-based platforms. While they look different on the surface, both operate on the same agency model. Clinicians are supplied through a centralized system where the agency controls pricing, contracts, and placement terms. Technology may speed up matching, and recruiters may manage relationships, but ownership and decision-making remain with the agency. The result is variable access to short-term and contract clinicians — with higher costs, limited transparency, and reduced control for healthcare owners over time.

What This Means for Your Business

1
Higher Costs That Add Up Quickly

Agency rates include built-in margins. Even when hourly rates look manageable, repeated use quietly inflates your total staffing spend — without improving retention or continuity.

2
Speed Over Stability

Agencies are optimized to fill shifts and their pocketbooks, not to build long-term clinician relationships. That often means:

  • Constantly onboarding new clinicians
  • Less familiarity with your residents or patients
  • More time spent managing transitions

3
Limited Control Over Who You Hire

With agencies, clinician selection is typically managed for you — not by you. You may not know:

  • Who else that clinician is working for
  • How often they rotate through different sites
  • Whether they’ll be available again
At The Traveling Therapist, we give you clinician contact details after you match, you can contact the clinician directly and ask all these questions yourself!
4
The Extra Problem with Brick-and-Mortar Agencies

Traditional bricks & mortar agencies add another layer of friction:

  • Placement speed now depends on recruiter availability in the office to answer calls/emails
  • Pricing and margins are harder to see or compare
  • Physical agencies have overhead, rent, electricity, etc. to pay for, which trickles down to their clients via their spreads/rates

Over time, this often results in higher cumulative costs and less consistency, even when the service feels “hands-on.”

Case Study: TempStars

Model Overview:

TempStars is a digital platform commonly used by Canadian dental clinics to fill last-minute dental temping shifts. For single, short-notice gaps, the platform can provide access to available clinicians through individual shift postings and standardized rules. However, clinics with frequent, recurring, or multi-day staffing needs often experience growing operational friction.

Where Clinics Start to Feel the Friction:
Manual clinic owner workflows

Because each shift must be posted, filled, and managed independently, staffing quickly becomes a manual, high-touch process. Clinics cannot bundle days, pre-book clinicians across multiple shifts, or easily build repeat working relationships.

Rigid cancellation process & Subjective Rating System

Ratings and cancellation outcomes—while consistent—can feel rigid, sometimes reducing future clinician availability after one-off issues without meaningful context. Clinicians with higher ratings or “elite” status get priority access to shifts - leaving clinic owners with an incomplete pool of potential candidates. Some clinicians have expressed frustration with the ratings system, limiting their ability to get shifts.

Cognitive load caused by distributed policies

Key details around cancellations, payments, and fees are documented, but spread across multiple support pages, meaning full cost and risk clarity often appears after an issue occurs or an invoice is received. For operators managing multiple locations or provinces, inconsistent geographic coverage throughout Canada, including Quebec further limits scalability and usability of the platform.

Why this matters:

TempStars works best for isolated, one-off shifts. Clinics looking to reduce administrative effort, improve continuity, and gain clearer upfront visibility into costs and policies often find the model adds complexity over time — gaps The Traveling Therapist is built specifically to eliminate.
