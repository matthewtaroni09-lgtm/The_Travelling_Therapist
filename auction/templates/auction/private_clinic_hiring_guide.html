The Workforce Reality Clinics Are Facing

1. Workforce Availability Is Tightening

Overtime & Supply Pressure

In 2024, 1 in 5 health-occupation employees worked overtime; among nurses and allied health professionals (including physiotherapists, OTs), this rises to ~25%

Reliance on internationally educated physiotherapists increased from 14% (2014) → 24% (2023)

What this means for clinics:
Clinicians are stretched thin, less flexible, and less available for extra shifts. Physiotherapy, chiropractic, RMT, and osteopathy clinics draw from the same limited talent pool. Higher overtime and international reliance signal reduced surplus capacity — fewer clinicians are available to “pick up extra hours” when you need them.

4. Staffing Gaps Last Longer Than Expected

90+ Days: 52.5% Under 90 Days: 47.5%

Vacancy Duration

Health occupation vacancy rate: 2.1% (2016) → 5.8% (2024)

52.5% of nursing & allied-health vacancies last 90+ days

Acording to Statistics Canada:

"vacancy: A job (full-time, part-time, permanent, temporary, casual, or seasonal) is vacant if:

  • it is vacant on the reference date (first day of the month) or will become vacant during the month
  • there are tasks to be carried out during the month for the job in question
  • the employer is actively recruiting outside the organization to fill the job."
What this means for clinics:
Hiring delays directly translate to:
  • Reduced appointment availability
  • Lost revenue
  • Increased pressure on remaining staff
