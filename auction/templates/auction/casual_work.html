70.7% 62.7% 30,776 13,500+

Approximately 70.7% of all licensed physiotherapists (PTs) in Canada are female.

Close to two-thirds (62.7%) of the entire Canadian PT workforce is younger than 45. This explicitly proves the overwhelming majority is actively navigating primary family-building and early-parenting years.

Out of a national supply of 30,776 licensed PTs, this represents an active cohort of roughly 13,500+ female practitioners sitting right in the demographic window where balancing career with family demands casual contract work.

Panel B — Licensing for Retirees & Parents

Most provincial regulatory bodies enforce a strict Competency Practice Hour Minimum to maintain an active independent license.

1,200 HOURS / 5 YRS

The College of Physiotherapists of Ontario (CPO) and most Canadian provinces strictly mandate a minimum of 1,200 practice hours within the previous five years to renew or maintain a PT license.

If a clinician on parental leave or transitioning into retirement falls below this threshold, they lose their license. Re-entry requires working hundreds of hours under strict direct supervision alongside an on-site clinical practice assessment.

Sources: CHCPBC, College of Physiotherapists of Alberta, SCPT, College of Physiotherapists of Ontario, OPPQ, Nova Scotia College of Physiotherapists.

Expanding the Supply Pool: The Nurse & PSW Burnout

While mid-to-late-career Physiotherapists face operational friction, Registered Nurses (RNs/RPNs) and Personal Support Workers (PSWs) face a state of chronic and systemic institutional burnout. In Canada, institutional healthcare environments rely on heavy scheduling frameworks: 12-hour rotating shifts, mandatory overtime, chronic understaffing, and high-acute emotional stress.

This environment creates a massive pool of highly skilled, credentialed "non-customers." These are nurses and PSWs who love patient care but are actively fleeing full-time institutional employment to save their own mental and physical health.

How The Traveling Therapist Fixes Burnout
TTT serves as the ultimate pressure-relief for these professionals. Instead of forcing them to abandon their hard-earned healthcare credentials entirely, TTT bridges them into the casual, low-stress outpatient and community health markets.

TTT strips away the administrative work and the scheduling difficulties. A nurse can step down from a chaotic hospital floor but still use TTT to claim a simple, low-intensity shift administering specialized infusions at a local private clinic, or a clean 3-day coverage contract. It completely reframes healthcare work from an exhausting obligation to an autonomous, lifestyle-first choice.

Overcoming the "Caseload Consistency" Hurdle

The Challenge: Clinic owners frequently push back against rotating or casual clinicians, citing the importance of clinical "consistency" for patient recovery outcomes.

TTT overcomes this by educating clinics that our platform does not mean random daily clinician churn. TTT is designed for structured block-bookings, ensuring clinics maintain high clinical consistency. If a clinician is a good fit, the clinic can onboard them to a full/part-time permanent position with zero additional charges from TTT!

Use Case 1: Ultra-Short / Emergency

Low-friction matching to patch immediate operational holes caused by sudden sickness, unexpected clinician callouts, short-term vacations, or abrupt leaves of absence.

Use Case 2: Recurring Blocks ("Try Before You Buy")

Securing a single clinician for a regular recurring block (e.g., every Tuesday & Thursday for 6 weeks). Maintains consistency and serves as a low-risk evaluation period for a potential permanent hire off-platform.

Use Case 3: Long-Term Structured Coverage

Standardized placements designed for extended clinic vacancies, such as 12-month maternity leave backfills, prolonged medical leaves, or dedicated extended clinician presence.

Use Case 4: Seasonal Surge & Burnout Relief

Deploying contract clinicians during predictable seasonal peaks like summer holiday blocks. Puts immediate support into the system, preventing the core full-time team from exhaustion.
